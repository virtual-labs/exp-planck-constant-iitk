<b>Procedure to run the simulation : </b><br>

1. Click on "ENABLE POWER" button to start the experiment<br>
   - This will activate all the circuit components and start the instruction panel<br>

2. Click on "Place LED" button to place an LED on the canvas<br>
   - You'll need to click on the canvas area to position the LED<br>

3. Click on "Battery" button to place a battery on the canvas<br>
   - Position the battery appropriately on the canvas<br>

4. Click on "Ammeter" button to place an ammeter on the canvas<br>
   - This will measure the current flow through the circuit<br>

5. Click on "Voltmeter" button to place a voltmeter on the canvas<br>
   - This will measure the voltage across the LED<br>

6. Click on "Connect" button to connect the components<br>
   - Click on the terminals of the components to create connections<br>
   - Connect: Battery (+) → Ammeter → LED (+) → LED (-) → Battery (-)<br>

7. Select LED Color from the dropdown menu<br>
   - Choose from: Red, Blue, Green, or Yellow<br>

8. Click "Click" button next to the color selection<br>
   - This will enable the current measurement button<br>

9. Click "Find Here" button to get the current value<br>
   - This will show the current flowing through the LED<br>

10. Use the voltage slider to adjust the voltage<br>
    - Slide from 0 to 5V to find the threshold voltage where the LED just starts glowing<br>

11. *Observe the LED* to determine the threshold voltage (V₀)<br>
    - Note the voltage at which the LED first emits light<br>

12. *Record the data* in the observations table<br>
    - Enter the LED color, threshold voltage (V₀), and wavelength (λ) for each color<br>

13. *Repeat steps 2-12* for different LED colors (Red, Yellow, Green, Blue)<br>

14. *Click "Calculate Data" button* after entering all observations<br>
    - This will calculate 1/λ values for the graph<br>

15. *Click "Draw Graph" button* to plot the graph<br>
    - This will create a graph of V₀ vs 1/λ<br>

16. *Enter coordinates* in the "Enter First Coordinate" and "Enter Second Coordinate" fields<br>
    - Use two points from your graph to calculate the slope<br>

17. *Click "Find Slope" button* to calculate the slope<br>
    - This will give you the slope of the linear plot<br>

18. *Enter the calculated slope* in the "Enter Slope" field<br>

19. *Click "Calculate" button* to find Planck's constant<br>
    - This will calculate h = (slope × e) / c<br><br>
